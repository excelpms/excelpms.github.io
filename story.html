How PMS began

It began with less repetition.
It grew toward the whole project.

From a file organizer to a project management framework connecting plans, execution, progress and reports—shaped by a petrochemical equipment project manager’s practical work and continued development.

01 / The project reality

Before the decision, gather the information.

Which drawing revision was submitted? Has the client returned comments? Is the document holding up procurement ready for the next activity? After a schedule revision, do the curve and current report still use the same data?

The answers may exist, but they are scattered across emails, folders, registers and schedules. An issue requires filing, registration and a transmittal. A return means updating revisions, Codes and status. Reporting day means assembling the information again.

02 / A practical starting point

Make organizing files a little easier.

The developer of PMS is a project manager in the petrochemical equipment industry who faces these problems in practice. The starting point was not a complete management system, but a familiar tool: could Excel make filing and registration less laborious?

The tool began with document organization. Practical use gradually brought revisions, submissions, reviews, transmittals and correspondence into its scope.

03 / What needed to connect

Where the file is was only the first question.

The next questions were how far work had progressed and what would be affected next. Engineering reviews influence procurement and fabrication; task dates affect planned progress; actual records need to reach dashboards and reports.

As long as those activities maintained separate data, repeated preparation and inconsistency remained. Document control needed to connect with plans and progress through clear data relationships.

04 / Growing through practice

From daily records to project control.

Over years of practical use, development and reflection, the original file organizer grew to include WBS planning, scheduling logic, document planning, VDR/GDR, transmittals, progress calculations, Dashboard, Lookahead and periodic reports.

PMS retains Excel's familiarity and flexibility. Teams can organize their WBS, calendars, progress rules and reporting content, while tracing summaries back to everyday work.

Less effort assembling information. A clearer basis for timely decisions.

PMS does not replace a project manager's experience. It aims to bring that experience and the relevant information together sooner: daily records support project control, and project control supports decisions.
